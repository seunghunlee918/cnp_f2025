# Conflict and Peace (Fall 2025, NTHU)

This is the repository containing the slides (pdf), codes to compile these slides (tex), and other course materials used for the Conflict and Peace class in Fall 2025 semester at Taipei School of Economics, National Tsing Hua University. 

This class covers topics in political economy of development, with emphasis on economics of conflict and crime, public economics in developing countries, and institutional economics. The course is organized around four key questions

- How do economic and institutional forces induce entities to engage in conflict and crime?
- How do conflict and crime affect individuals' opportunities and the development of institutions?
- How do individuals decide to resist or migrate away from conflicts?
- How do the institutions that prevent conflict and sustain peace emerge?

Specifically, the course covers the economic, political, and institiutional origins of conflict, political economy of organized crime, the consequence of violence on human capital accumulation and development, collective action problems, forced migration, and the fiscal and personnel dimensions of state capacity. 

The course is aimed at first-year PhD (and prepared MA) students in economics and political science. It assumes familiarity with statistical inference and post-graduate level micro/macroeconomics. Each week is built around  discussion of 5-7 seminal empirical papers that cover both the key theoretical frameworks and the causal evidence based on rigorous empirical studies. 

The materials are shared for reference and teaching purposes. You are welcome to adapt them with attribution. Please note that the reading list and assigned papers remain the intellectual property of their respective authors and publishers.
